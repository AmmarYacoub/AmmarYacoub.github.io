Helping students connect engineering theory with working hardware is an important part of my work as a researcher and educator. I am pleased to share [SMU Lyle’s July 2, 2026 news feature](https://www.smu.edu/lyle/latest-at-lyle/news/smu-lyle-ai-autonomous-systems-labs), which highlights the new laboratories I helped develop at Southern Methodist University.

## Helping build a hands-on learning environment

The article recognizes my role in laboratory development and curriculum design. My contributions included overseeing equipment installation and configuration, preparing the space for teaching and research, and organizing the technical demonstrations presented during the opening celebration.

This work drew on my background in industrial automation and my experience teaching engineering laboratories. It brought together system integration, technical problem-solving, and a question that matters to me as an educator: how can students use equipment to build a deeper understanding of engineering?

## Connecting control, robotics, and artificial intelligence

Developed in partnership with Quanser, the Lyle Applied Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and the Dynamic Systems, Controls, Robotics & Mechatronics Labs support experimentation with autonomous vehicles, drones, robotic systems, and digital twins. These facilities connect simulation with physical platforms and create opportunities for learning across engineering disciplines.

That connection reflects my approach to research in nonlinear control and haptics: developing models and algorithms, implementing them on hardware, and evaluating their behavior through experiments.

## Looking ahead

I am proud to have contributed to this collaborative effort. My hope is that students will use these spaces to ask questions, test ideas, and gain confidence connecting mathematical concepts with physical systems.
