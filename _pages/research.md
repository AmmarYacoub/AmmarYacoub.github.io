My research connects **mechanical design, dynamic modeling, and feedback control** to develop robotic systems that interact with people. In the Systems Lab at SMU, I focus on haptic devices and the challenges of delivering precise force feedback with pneumatic actuators.

## Haptic interfaces for virtual palpation

The SMU Haptic Glove is a seven-degree-of-freedom, pneumatically actuated device developed for virtual palpation in 3D elastography medical imaging. My work investigates its mechanical design, finger and glove workspaces, and kinematic relationships between actuator positions and fingertip locations.

I also study fingertip position estimation using embedded sensors and OptiTrack cameras, connecting analytical models with experimental measurements.

## Force control of pneumatic actuators

Small pneumatic cylinders offer a useful force-to-weight ratio for wearable haptic systems, but friction, air compressibility, and uncertain dynamics make accurate force tracking difficult.

My work explores static friction modeling and compensation, sliding mode control, online adaptation, and adaptive super-twisting control. These methods are evaluated experimentally on small pneumatic actuators to understand their tracking performance and robustness.
