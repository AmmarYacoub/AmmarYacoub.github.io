This paper presents the mechanical design and kinematic analyses of the SMU Haptic Glove, which incorporates seven degrees of freedom (DOF) that are pneumatically actuated and developed for 3D elastographic imaging virtual palpation. The two-dimensional workspace for each finger and the glove’s three-dimensional workspace are determined from the actuator positions using closed-form expressions based on the design geometry and are measured using embedded linear sensors. The proposed mechanism is analyzed using manipulability and singularity analyses, as well as force transmission analysis to derive the theoretical fingertip force boundary. The accuracy of fingertip location, obtained from the linear sensors embedded in each finger substructure, is analyzed through two experiments. The first experiment compares fingertip positions for the index finger and thumb substructures with those recorded using a five-camera OptiTrack motion-capture system. The second experiment evaluates the overall accuracy of the glove in three dimensions via a size identification test.
